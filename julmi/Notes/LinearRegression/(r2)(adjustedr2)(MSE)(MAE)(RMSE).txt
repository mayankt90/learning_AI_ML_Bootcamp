**R² and Adjusted R² in Linear Regression**

---

### 📌 R² (Coefficient of Determination)
- **Definition**: Measures how well the independent variables explain the variation in the dependent variable.
- **Formula**:
  
  \[ R^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_{res}}{SS_{tot}} \]

  Where:
  - \( SS_{res} \): Sum of Squares of Residuals
  - \( SS_{tot} \): Total Sum of Squares

- **Interpretation**:
  - R² = 0: Model explains none of the variance.
  - R² = 1: Model explains all of the variance.
  - Higher R² = Better fit.

- **Use Case**:
  - When you want to evaluate how well your regression model fits the data.

---

### 📌 Adjusted R²
- **Definition**: Modified version of R² that adjusts for the number of predictors in the model.
- **Formula**:

  \[ \text{Adjusted } R^2 = 1 - \left( \frac{(1 - R^2)(n - 1)}{n - k - 1} \right) \]

  Where:
  - \( n \): Number of observations
  - \( k \): Number of predictors

- **Why Use It?**
  - Prevents overfitting by penalizing unnecessary predictors.
  - Helps compare models with different numbers of predictors.

- **Use Case**:
  - Use Adjusted R² instead of R² when comparing models with different numbers of independent variables.

---

### 📌 Mean Squared Error (MSE)
- **Definition**: Measures the average squared difference between actual and predicted values.
- **Formula**:

  \[ MSE = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2 \]

- **Interpretation**:
  - Lower MSE means better model performance.
  - Sensitive to outliers due to squaring.

- **Use Case**:
  - Evaluate regression model performance.
  - Preferable when large errors are particularly undesirable.

---

### 📌 Mean Absolute Error (MAE)
- **Definition**: Measures the average absolute difference between actual and predicted values.
- **Formula**:

  \[ MAE = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^{n} |y_i - \hat{y}_i| \]

- **Interpretation**:
  - Lower MAE indicates better predictive accuracy.
  - More robust to outliers compared to MSE.

- **Use Case**:
  - Use when you want a simple interpretation of average model error.

---

### 📌 Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)
- **Definition**: Square root of MSE; brings error back to the original units.
- **Formula**:

  \[ RMSE = \sqrt{MSE} \]

- **Interpretation**:
  - Lower RMSE means better performance.
  - Penalizes large errors more than MAE.

- **Use Case**:
  - Common in regression tasks where interpretability in original units is important.
